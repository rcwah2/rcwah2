## Enterprise AI Problems This Work Addresses

Enterprise AI challenges are rarely isolated technology problems. They often involve investment decisions, organizational readiness, data, governance, cybersecurity, vendors, accountability, change, operations, and measurable business value.

The work in these repositories is intended to address recurring enterprise AI problems by connecting governance and risk with implementation and operational execution.

| Enterprise AI Pain Point | Intended Business Outcome | Evidence / Repository |
|---|---|---|
| AI initiatives move toward production without sufficient enterprise readiness | Make readiness, dependencies, controls, evidence, and decision criteria visible before the organization accepts additional business exposure. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |
| AI risks and impacts are identified too late | Identify material impacts, risk conditions, affected stakeholders, and control gaps earlier in the lifecycle. | [AI Governance Portfolio](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-governance-portfolio); [AI System Inventory Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-system-inventory-template) |
| Accountability and decision rights are unclear | Clarify ownership, escalation paths, risk acceptance, production authorization, and who has authority to make consequential decisions. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model); [AI Governance Portfolio](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-governance-portfolio) |
| AI investments are not clearly connected to business value | Connect AI investment decisions to expected outcomes, value realization, capacity, risk, and decisions to accelerate, redesign, defer, or stop initiatives. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |
| AI pilots struggle to scale into enterprise operations | Connect experimentation with the additional data, governance, cybersecurity, change, operational, vendor, and production capabilities needed for enterprise deployment. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |
| Governance is disconnected from implementation | Translate governance expectations into real activities, evidence, controls, decision gates, and operating processes. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model); [AI Governance Crosswalk](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-governance-crosswalk) |
| Organizations lack visibility into their AI systems and dependencies | Improve system inventory, ownership, classification, data provenance, autonomy visibility, and governance traceability. | [AI System Inventory Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-system-inventory-template); [AI Governance Portfolio](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-governance-portfolio) |
| Third-party AI introduces risks the organization does not directly control | Strengthen vendor due diligence, operational requirements, contractual expectations, cybersecurity, incident response, and agentic AI controls. | [AI Vendor Due Diligence Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-vendor-due-diligence-template) |
| Data quality, access, provenance, and architecture limit AI readiness | Make data readiness and information dependencies part of implementation planning rather than discovering them late in delivery. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model); [AI System Inventory Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-system-inventory-template) |
| Organizational change and workflow redesign are addressed too late | Treat leadership alignment, workflow change, roles, training, adoption, and reinforcement as lifecycle activities rather than post-deployment tasks. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |
| AI initiatives compete for scarce funding, data, platforms, talent, and business capacity | Improve portfolio prioritization and expose cross-initiative dependencies before resources are committed. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |
| Organizations struggle to determine whether deployed AI continues to deliver acceptable risk and value | Extend governance beyond deployment through monitoring, incident response, reassessment, reauthorization, and value realization. | [AI Implementation Reference Model](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) |

**The objective is not more governance documentation. It is better enterprise decisions about what should move forward, under what conditions, with what controls, and how value will be measured after deployment.**

These themes reflect recurring enterprise AI challenges addressed across the NIST AI Risk Management Framework and current enterprise AI governance and transformation research, including work from Deloitte and BCG. The specific applicability and priority of each issue will vary by organization, use case, risk profile, and regulatory environment.

## Repositories

### Reference Model
- [From AI Pilot to Enterprise Readiness](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-implementation-reference-model) — Phased implementation reference model with 6 phases, decision gates, and framework alignment. The hub that ties all governance artifacts together.

### AI Governance Tools
- [AI Vendor Due Diligence Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-vendor-due-diligence-template) — Three-part governance pack: due diligence questionnaire, SOW/operational requirements, and contract clause checklist. Covers NIST AI RMF, ISO 42001, NIST SP 800-61/86, ISO 27037/27042/27043, and agentic AI controls (NIST AI 100-4/100-5, OWASP Top 10 for Agentic Applications).
- [AI System Inventory Template](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-system-inventory-template) — YAML/JSON schema for documenting AI systems. Includes agentic AI fields: autonomy classification, tool/API access, identity model, kill switch, behavioral monitoring.
- [AI Governance Crosswalk](https://github.com/rcwah2/ai-governance-crosswalk) — Practical implementation guide for organizations adopting both NIST AI RMF and ISO 42001. Shared artifacts, evidence mapping, and review cadence alignment.
